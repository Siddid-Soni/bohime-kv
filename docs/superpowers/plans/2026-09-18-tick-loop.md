# The shard-count read cliff was the client, not the tick loop (2026-09-18)

Not a milestone. `2026-09-18-write-throughput.md` closed with "the shared tick
loop, not durability, is the next ceiling", pointing at a read control arm that
fell **14.4k → 8.9k → 2.2k op/s** across 1 → 8 → 64 shards while touching no
disk, and at a write curve that turned over between 8 and 64 for what looked
like the same reason. This session went looking for a second instance of the
M11 bug — something O(groups) on a per-message path.

## The headline

**There is no per-group cost in the driver loop. Draining all 64 groups costs
40 µs and accounts for 0.2–0.5% of the loop.** The cliff was entirely in
`kv-client`: a 50 ms `RETRY_PAUSE` taken at the end of *every* retry round,
including rounds that ended holding an actionable leader hint. A fresh client
has to learn one leader per shard it touches, so the cost was
`shards × 50 ms` spread over that client's first pass — and the benchmark
built fresh clients for every arm and gave them 400 operations to amortise it
over.

One line moved. The read curve went flat:

| shards, get @ 32 clients | 1 | 8 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|
| before | 16045 op/s | 12133 | **3581** |
| after | 16876 op/s | 14283 | **15308** |
| p99 before | 2.68 ms | 52.64 ms | **53.98 ms** |
| p99 after | 2.94 ms | 3.96 ms | **4.10 ms** |

4.3× at 64 shards, and a 13× better tail. The residual 1→64 ratio is 1.10×,
inside this box's run-to-run spread.

Given a long enough run and a real hit rate, reads now **rise** with shard
count, which is the property M11 exists to deliver and which no measurement
had yet shown. 32 clients, 1000 reads each, every one a hit, `/tmp` so the
prefill finishes this side of an hour:

| shards | 1 | 8 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|
| get op/s | 3767 | 13219 | **14224** |
| p50 | 7.92 ms | 2.33 ms | 1.99 ms |

3.8× over the range. The 1-shard point is the floor for the same reason the
1-shard *write* point is: one group is one serialized driver's worth of
ReadIndex rounds, and there is nothing to spread.

**The write turnover between 8 and 64 is the fsync budget and nothing else.**
The same sweep with `BOHIME_BENCH_DIR=/tmp` — the same binary, `fdatasync`
removed by the filesystem — gives **83 → 615 → 3295 writes/s, 39.7× rising,
no turnover at all**. On disk it is 77 → 228 → 142, and at 8 shards
`228 × 3 nodes = 684` fsyncs/s against a device measured at 593–742/s. The box
is saturated at 8 shards; at 64 the per-group group-commit batch is 32 clients
over 64 groups, so there is nothing left to coalesce and each write costs its
full fsyncs again.

## Ruling out the confound first, as instructed

The benchmark held clients at 32 while shard count rose, which at 64 shards is
half a client per shard. Two experiments separated methodology from mechanism,
both on the read arm because it has no disk in it.

**Scaling offered load recovered most of it but not all.** Peak throughput
(`BOHIME_BENCH_CLIENTS=512`): 53457 → 46275 → 32431 op/s. 1.65×, against 4.5×
at a fixed 32 clients. So a real effect survived scaling, and the fixed-32
sweep was overstating it by ~2.7×.

**Amortising the cold start erased it.** The same 32 clients given 4000
operations each instead of 400: 17416 → 17197 → 13928 op/s, and p99 2.70 →
2.89 → 7.92 ms. A degradation that shrinks as each client does *more* work is
a per-client-lifetime cost, not a per-operation one — which is what pointed at
client state rather than at the driver.

Both together said: the effect is real, it is not the offered-load confound,
and it is not per-operation. That is a different shape from the M11 bug, which
was strictly per-message.

## The mechanism, and how it was measured

### The driver loop was instrumented and exonerated first

A temporary probe in `Driver::run` timed every pass, separating tick passes
(which drain **every** group) from message passes (which drain one), and wrote
a line a second. Against a 64-shard cluster under the read load:

```
groups=64 tick_passes=50 tick_mean_us=42.3 tick_max_us=108.3 tick_share=0.2% msg_passes=9210  msg_mean_us=2.44
groups=64 tick_passes=50 tick_mean_us=43.4 tick_max_us=112.1 tick_share=0.2% msg_passes=25173 msg_mean_us=2.31
groups=64 tick_passes=50 tick_mean_us=53.5 tick_max_us=129.6 tick_share=0.3% msg_passes=40105 msg_mean_us=2.05
```

Ticking and draining all 64 groups costs **42 µs**, against a 20 ms interval —
0.2% of one core, with the tick arm hitting its full 50 ticks a second
throughout, so `select!` was not starving it either. A per-message drain costs
**2 µs** and does not grow with group count. `reconcile_peers`, the M11
offender, is already gated behind `RECONCILE_EVERY_TICKS = 50` and runs once a
second.

The probe has been removed; `driver.rs` is byte-identical to what this session
started with.

### What the latency distribution said

The read arm's p50 barely moved across the sweep (1.79 → 1.81 → 1.45 ms) while
p99 jumped to **53.5 ms** and max sat at ~58 ms. A flat median with a tail
pinned just above a round number is a fixed-size stall on a fraction of
requests, not per-operation overhead. 53.5 ms is `RETRY_PAUSE` plus a round
trip, and `RETRY_PAUSE` is `crates/kv-client/src/lib.rs:69`,
`Duration::from_millis(50)`.

The arithmetic closes exactly. At 64 shards a cold client touching 64 shards
over 300 operations pays ~42 pauses (it guesses right about a third of the
time, there being three nodes):

| shards | predicted arm time | measured |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.03 s pause + 0.54 s work = 0.57 s | 0.60 s |
| 8 | 0.35 s + 0.35 s = 0.70 s | 0.79 s |
| 64 | 2.1 s + 0.31 s = 2.4 s | 2.35 s |

### The bug

Both retry loops in `kv-client` ended a round with an unconditional
`tokio::time::sleep(RETRY_PAUSE)`. But a round can end four ways, and only one
of them is the case that pause was written for:

- every candidate refused and none named a leader — no information, pace
  yourself;
- a `NotLeader` carrying a `leader_hint`, already stored in `hinted`;
- a `NotHosted` whose replica list `redirect` turned into a `hinted`;
- a transport error.

The middle two are a node having just answered and said *where to go*. Sleeping
50 ms before taking up an answer already in hand is the pause spent on the one
case it was never for. In a healthy cluster a follower always knows its leader,
so a cold client's first request to each shard hits this with probability 2/3.

The fix is that a round which produced a hint does not pause — bounded so it
cannot become a spin:

```rust
async fn pace(&self, hinted: Option<NodeId>, followed: &mut BTreeSet<NodeId>) {
    if let Some(id) = hinted && followed.insert(id) {
        return;
    }
    tokio::time::sleep(RETRY_PAUSE).await;
}
```

Each node is worth one free hop per request. Two nodes pointing at each other —
what a client sees for a moment after a failover — gets its free hops and then
goes back to pacing, so the 6-second retry budget cannot be burned at loopback
speed. `get` and `write` both call it.

## Tests

`crates/kv-client/src/tests/redirect.rs`, three tests, built on a fake
two-node cluster: real `tonic` servers implementing `KvService` and
`AdminService`, each node scripted to answer a `Get` with a hint, a hintless
refusal, or the value.

- `a_leader_hint_is_followed_without_the_retry_pause` — the regression test.
  Node 1 says "ask node 2", node 2 answers. Two loopback round trips, so it
  must finish inside one `RETRY_PAUSE`.
- `nodes_pointing_at_each_other_still_pace_themselves` — node 1 hints 2, node 2
  hints 1, forever. Counts `Get`s over 400 ms and asserts the loop did not
  spin. This is what the `followed` set is for, and it fails against the naive
  "never pause on a hint".
- `a_hintless_refusal_still_pauses` — the case the pause exists for, pinned so
  the fix cannot be widened into removing it.

**The scale the regression test has teeth at is one redirect.** It does not
need 64 shards, or 64 of anything: the bug is one sleep on one code path, and
one hop through it exposes the whole 50 ms. Verified by mutation — with the fix
reverted it fails with

```
following a hint took 59.940103ms, which is at least one RETRY_PAUSE (50ms):
the redirect slept before taking up an answer it already had
```

which is the pause plus ~10 ms of honest debug-build work: a 5× margin against
the 50 ms threshold, so it is not a timing-flaky test. With the fix it runs in
27 ms.

This is a better-natured guard than M11's, which needs 256 shards before the
in-process harness can feel the cost. The difference is that this bug is a
fixed latency rather than an amount of work, so it does not need scale to
become visible — it needed a *cold client*, which the benchmark had and no test
did.

## The benchmark's own bug: the read arm never hit

`get (control)` has been reading keys that were never written, for its entire
existence. Both arms seeded one `Rng` per client and took the key from it, but
`put_load` also drew 256 bytes of value from that same stream *before* each
key, so the two walked it at strides of 33 and 1 and never agreed after the
first draw. Verified directly:

```
puts ["bench/1/0/0a57f890c936fb35", "bench/1/0/352e93d0a08c93b0", ...]
gets ["bench/1/0/8f8f22bc585c9ce6", "bench/1/0/98f5b9b99b03bf7a", ...]
```

The module comment said "the read arm reads back phase-1 keys, so every one is
a hit". Every one was a miss: a real ReadIndex quorum round trip and a real
keydir lookup, but never a value off the disk. **Every `get (control)` number
published before today, including the 14.4k/8.9k/2.2k that prompted this work,
is a miss rate of 100%.** As a bound on "how much of the write number is the
client, the runtime and gRPC" that is still a fair control — it just is not
what it claimed, and it never exercised M11.5's wait-free read path end to end.

`keys(c, phase)` is now its own generator that values do not draw from, so the
two arms agree by construction, and `get_load` asserts on a miss rather than
counting it. The read arm now needs the write arm's keys, so a `get`-only run
does an untimed prefill.

## Changes to the benchmark harness

- `BOHIME_BENCH_ARMS=put,get` selects arms. A shard sweep hunting a per-group
  cost wants the read arm and should not spend a minute a point on `fdatasync`.
- `BOHIME_BENCH_CLIENTS` takes a comma-separated list and sweeps it inside each
  shard count, which is what turns "is this the methodology?" into one run.
  Each client count gets its own key phase.
- `keys()`/`get_load` as above.

## Things that came out differently from CLAUDE.md, DESIGN.md or the handoffs

**`2026-09-18-write-throughput.md`'s closing section names the wrong cause.**
"This is per-group cost in `Driver::run`, and it is the same shape M11 already
found once" — it is not. The driver loop spends 0.2% of itself on all 64
groups. The read cliff was `kv-client`, and the write turnover is the fsync
budget the same document had already identified one section earlier.

**The read arm was never a read.** See above. The benchmark's module header
claims a hit rate it never had.

**The M11 lesson holds in a new place.** 402 tests passed with a 50 ms sleep on
the hot path of every cold client, because no test ever built a client, pointed
it at a follower and timed the redirect. The suite tests that a redirect
*happens*; it never tested what one costs.

## What is now in front

**The write path is fsync-bound at 8 shards and load-collapsing past it.**
Measured after the fix, real disk: at 8 shards throughput *falls* with more
offered load — 245 op/s at 32 clients, 167 at 128 (p99 2722 ms), and at 512
clients the driver's bounded request channel sheds `ResourceExhausted` for
longer than the client's whole 6-second budget and the benchmark aborts. At 64
shards: 137 at 32 clients, 127 at 128 (p99 5145 ms). That is congestion
collapse, and the honest next question is admission control — the client
treats `ResourceExhausted` as a transport failure and retries the same node,
where it is backpressure and should be paced or shed to another replica.

**Group commit has nothing to batch at high shard counts.** The tmpfs control
scales 39.7×, so the machinery is fine; on disk, 32 clients over 64 groups is
0.5 writes per group per drain and the batch is a batch of one. What would help
is fewer logs, not more batching — one shared log per node with per-group
segments, which is a design change and not a tuning one.

**AppendEntries pipelining is still the single-shard answer.** 77 op/s at 1
shard, p50 404 ms, on a device doing 600+ fsync/s. Unchanged by any of this and
still gated on the nemesis sweep.
